Pork Sinigang Recipe

Home

Description:

Pork sinigang is a beloved Filipino tamarind-based soup defined by its sharply sour, vibrant, and deeply savory broth.

Ingredients:

  • Pork: 1 kg pork belly, ribs, or shoulder (cut into chunks)
  • Tamarind Base: 1 pack (20-40g) sinigang mix (or fresh tamarind fruit/paste)
  • Water Spinach: 1 bunch of kang kong (leaves and tender stems)
  • Radish: 1 medium daikon radish (labanos), sliced into rounds
  • String Beans: 1 bunch of sitaw (yardlong beans), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • Eggplant: 1 long eggplant (talong), sliced obliquely
  • Okra: 5 to 6 pieces okra, ends trimmed
  • Tomatoes: 2 large tomatoes, wedged
  • Onion: 1 large purple onion, wedged
  • Taro: 2 to 3 small taro roots (gabi), peeled and halved (to thicken the broth)
  • Green Long Chili: 2 to 3 pieces siling haba (finger chilies)
  • Fish Sauce: 2 to 3 tablespoons (patis, to taste)
  • Water: 6 to 8 cups water

Steps:

  1. Build the broth base: In a large pot, combine the pork chunks, tomatoes, onions, taro roots (gabi), and water. Bring the mixture to a rolling boil.
  2. Simmer the pork: Lower the heat to medium-low, cover, and let it simmer for about 45 to 60 minutes. The taro roots should begin to break down, naturally thickening the broth, while the pork becomes melt-in-your-mouth tender.
  3. Sour the soup: Stir in the sinigang mix (or fresh tamarind juice) and the fish sauce. Taste the broth and adjust the sourness or saltiness to your personal preference.
  4. Layer the vegetables: Add the hard vegetables first—the radish, okra, and eggplant. Simmer for 3 to 5 minutes. Next, add the string beans (sitaw) and the long green chilies (siling haba), cooking for another 3 minutes.
  5. Add the greens: Toss the water spinach (kang kong) leaves and tender stems on top. Turn off the burner, cover the pot, and let it steam for 1 to 2 minutes until the greens are perfectly vibrant and wilted.
